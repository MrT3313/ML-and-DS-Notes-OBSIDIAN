## Definition

An origin of the data arriving in a running system: what produced a record, before anything inside the system had a chance to check it. Four of them cover what a production system actually receives, and they are the structure of this note: data a user enters, data the system's own components emit, databases other teams in the company already run, and data bought from outside.

[[Open Data Repositories]] uses this phrase for a different thing, a place to go and download a public dataset when a project has none, and a reader who wanted that list wants that note rather than this one. Neither is a special case of the other: one is where to obtain a dataset before anything exists, the other is what keeps arriving once something is live.

## Formal statement

The four origins differ on properties that can be checked against a real system, which is what makes them four things rather than four names for one.

| Origin | Produced by | Malformed by default | When it has to be processed | Accountable for its quality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| User input | a person typing, uploading, choosing or pasting | yes | immediately, while the person waits | the receiving system, at the boundary, since nothing upstream is answerable |
| System generated | the system's own components: logs, service outputs, model predictions, behavioural events | rarely, the format is emitted by code under the same ownership | when convenient, so periodically | the team that owns the emitting code, which is the same team |
| Internal databases | other services and applications inside the company | rarely as a format, though the meaning of a field can be stale or contested | on whatever schedule the owning service publishes | the team that owns that service, which is not the consuming team |
| Third-party data | a vendor collecting about people who are not its own customers | unknown, and not checkable from inside | on the vendor's delivery schedule | the vendor by contract, the buyer in practice |

Two of those rows carry claims strong enough to be found false, and they are the ones worth stating outright rather than leaving in a cell.

**User input is malformed by default.** If it is even remotely possible for somebody to enter the wrong thing, somebody eventually will, so validation on this path is heavier than on any other and a parser that assumes well-formed input is a defect rather than a simplification. It also carries the tightest deadline of the four, and not because the data is worth more: the person who typed it is waiting for what comes back.

**System generated data inverts both properties.** Its shape is produced by code rather than by a person, so it is much less likely to arrive malformed, and nothing is blocked on it, so it does not have to be handled as it lands. Accumulating it and handling it on a schedule instead is [[Batch Processing]], and this row is the reason that mode exists at all on the ingest side.

One boundary cuts across the table: behavioural tracking of what a user clicked, which suggestion they took, which popup they dismissed and how long they lingered on a page is system generated by origin and user data by law, so it carries privacy obligations that the logs beside it do not. What those obligations require is not settled here, and of what is scheduled, [[DMLS]] chapter 11 is the likeliest place to bring it.

### What logging everything costs

Machine learning systems are hard to debug, because a broken one returns a prediction the same way a working one does, so the standing practice is to log everything that can be logged. That buys the ability to reconstruct what happened, and it has two costs that show up later rather than at the time.

The first is that the signal is lost in the noise. Having logged everything, nobody knows where to look, and the question stops being whether the evidence was captured and becomes whether it can be found.

The second is volume. The number of logs grows fast enough that storing them is a problem in its own right, separate from the problem they were kept to solve.

### First-party, second-party and third-party

Three labels for who did the collecting, and they say nothing about what the data contains.

- **First-party data** is what the company already collects about its own users or customers.
- **Second-party data** is what another company collects about its own customers and then makes available to you. It is first-party data that changed hands.
- **Third-party data** is collected about the public, who are not the collector's direct customers, and typically sold on after the vendor has cleaned and processed it.

### The third-party trade as of 2026

This market was reshaped after 2022 by platform policy rather than by any change in technology, so a claim about it needs a date attached to be worth anything.

Mobile identifiers went first. Apple's App Tracking Transparency requires an app to ask before tracking a user across other companies' apps and sites, and an app that is refused reads the advertising identifier as all zeros instead of a value; that has held since iOS 14.5 in April 2021. Google's version is softer in form and similar in effect: since a Google Play services update in late 2021, an Android user who opts out of ads personalization zeroes the advertising ID for every app that asks for it.

Third-party cookies did not follow. Safari has blocked them by default since Safari 13.1 and iOS 13.4 in March 2020. Firefox has shipped Total Cookie Protection to all users by default since June 2022, which partitions rather than blocks: each site gets its own cookie jar, so the same tracker's cookie on two sites cannot be joined. Chrome kept them, through a reversal and then a second change of plan. The removal was abandoned in July 2024 in favour of a user-choice design, that prompt was itself dropped on 22 April 2025 with Google stating it would keep its current approach and ship no new standalone prompt, and on 17 October 2025 most of the Privacy Sandbox APIs meant to replace cookie-based targeting were retired, Topics, Protected Audience, Attribution Reporting, Shared Storage and the rest, leaving CHIPS, FedCM and Private State Tokens. As of September 2026, third-party cookies still work in Chrome.

Pressure moved to the vendors instead. California's Delete Act puts data brokers on a public register held by the state privacy agency and routes consumer deletion through one state-run platform, open to residents from 1 January 2026, which registered brokers must begin honouring on 1 August 2026. That is a named list of who is in this business, which did not previously exist.

What none of this settles is whether the trade is smaller. The facts above are platform and regulatory policy and can be checked against the sources that published them; the size and shape of the resulting market is reported mostly by the industry selling into it, and I have found no primary source good enough to state a number here. The defensible reading is narrower: third-party data is harder to collect on mobile than it was, it is answerable to a named registry in at least one large jurisdiction, and the web mechanism everybody expected to disappear is the one still standing.

## Where it is used

[[Data Engineering]] is the standing job of getting data out of these four origins and into somewhere it can be used, which is why that role is defined by the origins it connects rather than by any single technique. [[Extract-Transform-Load]] is the machinery it does that with, and the extract step is exactly the step that reads a source in this sense, one connector per origin, gently enough not to damage the system it is reading.

[[System of Record]] is what separates the internal database row from the other three: an internal database is usually the authoritative copy of whatever it holds, so anything derived from it loses a disagreement with it, while user input, telemetry and purchased data are inputs to a record rather than the record.

[[Poor-Quality Data]] is the same trouble seen from the modelling end, errors, outliers and missing values in a training set, and the user input row above is the upstream origin of most of it. [[Batch Processing]] is the processing mode the system generated row makes available, available precisely because nobody is waiting on those records.

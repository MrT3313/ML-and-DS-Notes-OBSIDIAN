The machinery that stores, moves and serves data, kept as its own domain because every note in it would still be true if no model were ever fitted on anything it holds. That is the membership test, and it is the whole boundary: a note earns a place here when it is about where the data lives, how it gets there, who answers a query about it, and what breaks on the way. `Knowledge/Machine Learning/` owns what is learned from data; this domain owns the substrate that learning reads out of, which was built for reasons that have nothing to do with learning and would be built anyway. `Knowledge/Machine Learning/Operations/` owns operating a model that has shipped; this domain owns operating the services and the infrastructure underneath it, work that exists whether or not a model is among the things being operated. A note that only makes sense because there is a fitted model somewhere in the picture belongs to that domain and not here. `Knowledge/Systems Architecture/`, indexed at [[Systems Architecture]], is the domain this one leans on, and it owns the concepts that stand for any software system regardless of what it stores: how work is spread across machines, who owns the machines, who operates them, and how two processes that share no memory hand data to each other. That last one is the live boundary case, since [[Modes of Data Flow]], [[Request-Driven Communication]] and [[Event-Driven Communication]] were cut to that side rather than this one, the three modes being answers to a question about processes rather than about data, and [[Microservices]] having already held the request-driven half. The domain now holds twenty-four notes from two books. Thirteen originate in [[DDIA Ch01 Trade-Offs in Data Systems Architecture|DDIA chapter 1]], which surveys the architectural choices rather than the mechanisms; the other eleven arrived with [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals|DMLS chapter 3]], which works the other way round, and eight of the older thirteen gained material from it on the way through. What changed in kind is that the mechanisms are now here: the offset a layout puts an element at, the round trip a serialization format promises, the dependency a normal form forbids, what each letter of ACID does and does not guarantee, and the two processing contracts with the arithmetic of what each one costs. The vocabulary and the shape of the trade-offs are still the older half of this domain, and they are no longer all of it.

## Areas

- **`_Foundations/`** the vocabulary the rest of the domain is stated in. [[Data-Intensive Application]] is the axis the whole thing is organized around, an application whose primary difficulty is data management rather than arithmetic, with its four recurring challenges and the compute-intensive counterpart it is defined against. [[Data Source]] is what arrives and from where, four origins separated on properties a running system can be checked against: user input, malformed by default and on the tightest deadline of the four because the person who typed it is waiting; system generated telemetry, which inverts both of those and is the reason batch work is possible on the ingest side at all; the internal databases other teams already run; and bought third-party data, whose trade is dated to 2026 in the note because platform policy rather than technology reshaped it. [[Storage Engine]] is what holds the bytes once they land, the layer under the query interface rather than the database around it, with MySQL's per-table pluggable engines and the embedded key-value libraries that have no query language at all as the two demonstrations that the layers really do come apart. [[System of Record]] and [[Derived Data]] are the authority pair: the first holds the canonical copy and wins any disagreement by definition, the second is anything computable from it, redundant on purpose and rebuildable by rerunning the transformation, with the cache as the worked example of why a copy is allowed to exist only when a fallback path does.
- **`Representation/`** how data is organized logically, before any question about bytes arises. [[Data Model]] is the choice made before anything is written and the one that decides which questions the system can answer at all, with the relational, document and graph families set against each other on five axes: what a unit of storage is, how one unit refers to another, where the schema is enforced, what a cross-unit join costs, and which access pattern each is fast at. It also disposes of NoSQL as a category, a word settled on for a hashtag at a 2009 meetup, which says what a store does not use and therefore implies nothing about what it does. [[Relational Model]] is the family with a primary source under it, Codd 1970, where a relation is a set of tuples and the consequences all come from the word set: row order and attribute order carry no information, so the query language addresses attributes by name, and the optimizer is free to change the steps under a query that was never edited. [[Database Normalization]] is the design theory that exists only for that model, stated over functional dependencies rather than over tables, carrying the normal forms with 1NF's "atomic" wording replaced by the condition it can actually enforce, the three anomalies redundancy causes, and denormalization as the same trade run backwards. [[Structured Data]] is the upstream property the schema column of that table depends on, since a schema can be imposed only where there is one, and it carries the semi-structured middle case and the asymmetry worth keeping: a warehouse holding structured data is an entailment, a lake holding unstructured data is only an observation about what people put there.
- **`Encoding/`** how the same data is written down as bytes, which is the other half of the same question. [[Data Serialization]] fixes the round trip, that decoding an encoded value returns it, over exactly the values the format's own model admits, and settles everything else on three axes that are decided by inspecting a file rather than by reputation: whether a person can read it, whether every byte is part of a character, and whether a reader can reach one field without consuming everything before it. What a format does not fix is stated just as plainly, the schema, the compatibility rules and whether the writer's schema travels with the data. [[Row-Major and Column-Major Order]] is that third axis in full: the two offsets an element sits at, the fraction $k/n$ of a table a column store has to read with no row count anywhere in it, the reversal on whole rows and on appends, and the cache line underneath the usual unexplained assertion that sequential access is faster. It is also the note three others had been leaning on with nothing defining it, since [[Online Transaction Processing]], [[Online Analytical Processing]] and [[Hybrid Transactional-Analytical Processing]] each named row or columnar storage and none of them said what it was.
- **`Workloads/`** the shape of the traffic a system is built to take, which is the split every later choice follows from. [[Online Transaction Processing]] is many small requests touching few records each, answered while somebody waits, and it carries the eight-row comparison table that the whole folder turns on. [[Online Analytical Processing]] is the other half, few queries each sweeping millions of historical records and returning a summary, including the product-analytics variant that tightens the latency target without changing the workload class. [[Point Query]] is the exact-match lookup by key that defines the first of those, and [[Transaction]] is the bracket an application draws around a group of reads and writes so the database's guarantees attach to the group rather than the statements. Those guarantees now have a name and a bound: [[ACID]] takes each letter with what it promises set beside what it does not, dates the acronym to Haerder and Reuter in 1983, shows the C to be the one letter whose enforcement the database shares with the application, and puts BASE opposite it as the trade a partitioned system makes when it buys availability instead. Only the concurrency half is still deferred, since what two overlapping transactions do to each other waits on the isolation levels. [[Hybrid Transactional-Analytical Processing]] is the attempt to put both workloads back in one system, and its internals mostly explain why they were separated.
- **`Processing/`** whether work runs over data at rest or data in motion. The two notes here retire two of the five building blocks [[DDIA Ch01 Trade-Offs in Data Systems Architecture|DDIA chapter 1]] named without defining, which is the largest deliberate deferral that chapter recorded. [[Batch Processing]] is the bounded case: the input set is fixed before the run starts, so the output is a function of it, a failed run is rerun rather than repaired and no state is carried between runs, with the precondition that actually gets broken stated outright, that the job read nothing outside its input, and with freshness bounded below by the cadence rather than by the engineering, in $[R,\, T+R]$ for a job taking $R$ on an interval of $T$. [[Stream Processing]] is the unbounded case: no last record, so no final answer and only an answer as of a position in the sequence, state carried because the computation spans more records than the one in hand, and the payoff written as arithmetic, a window of $W$ days costing $Wr$ per run recomputed against $r + |S|$ maintained incrementally, a factor of $W$ bought at the price of repairing state instead of rerunning. It also takes the common claim that streaming is the less efficient of the two, checks it against what the engines actually document, and declines to make it.
- **`Analytics/`** where the copy goes once it has left the operational systems. [[Data Warehouse]] is the separate read-only store, conformed to one analysis schema on write and non-volatile so history accumulates instead of being overwritten, with a precise account of what it guarantees (isolation, one schema over many sources, rebuildability) and what it does not (freshness, authority, completeness). [[Data Lake]] is the same copy one step earlier, raw files with structure imposed at read time, defended by the sushi principle and undercut by the fact that nothing makes two files agree. [[Data Silo]] is the condition the warehouse exists to answer, one entity living in several systems under several identifiers with nothing holding the correspondence. [[Extract-Transform-Load]] is the running machinery of the copy, the only `method` note in the domain, carrying both letter orderings, what moves when the transform moves, and a failure list led by the upstream schema change nobody told the pipeline about.
- **`Operations/`** the role that joins the two halves of the domain to each other. [[Data Engineering]] is the work of integrating the operational and analytical systems and owning the pipelines, schedules and storage that integration runs on, described as a pair of roles rather than a technique: the data engineer, who owns the movement of data out of the system of record and the infrastructure it moves over, and the analytics engineer, a title datable to 2019, who applies software engineering discipline to the transformation code itself so it is version controlled, tested, documented and deployed rather than passed between people as SQL.

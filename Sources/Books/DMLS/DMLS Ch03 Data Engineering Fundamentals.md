## Thesis

The chapter is named for a role and is a survey of the substrate that role owns. It walks the substrate from the outside in, in one pass: where the data comes from before anything in the system has looked at it, what bytes it becomes once something has, how those bytes are laid out, what shape the records are organized into and what a query over them is allowed to ask for, which component actually puts them on a disk and finds them again, how they get from one process to another when the two share no memory, and when the computation over them runs. Four origins, six serialization formats, two layouts, three data models, two schema regimes, one engine layer, two workload shapes, one copy pipeline and three modes of flow, closing on two processing regimes. What keeps that from reading as a glossary is that nearly every section is the same move made on different material: a question with two answers and a price written on each side. Text against binary, row-major against column-major, relational against document against graph, structured against unstructured, transactional against analytical, request-driven against event-driven, batch against stream. Not one of those pairs has a winner, and in every case the thing that decides is a property of the traffic rather than a property of the technology: what one request touches, how many of the columns a query wants, whether the input ever ends, whether the consumer has to be running at the moment the producer sends. The pairs also compound rather than sitting side by side, and that is the part worth carrying away, because the workload shape picks the layout, the layout picks the format, the format decides what a reader has to consume before it can reach one field, and a decision taken at any one of those levels is paid for at the others. The chapter belongs to a machine learning book, which is the fact most worth stating plainly, since almost nothing in it needs a fitted model in the sentence. A batch job runs whether or not anything is ever fitted on what it produces, a relation is normalized for reasons Codd gave in 1970, and a broker's retention window bounds what a consumer can miss whatever that consumer was built for. That is [[Data Systems]]'s membership test in the words the domain states it in, and this material passes it almost entirely, which is why the run produced no new note in `Knowledge/Machine Learning/` at all and amended seven there instead. What the chapter contributes to the book it sits in is the vocabulary for talking about the data before anything is fitted on it, and the reason such a book needs that vocabulary is the one thing the chapter never says outright: every choice above is made by somebody else, upstream, for reasons that have nothing to do with any model, and the model gets whatever those choices left behind.

## Extracted

Where the data comes from, and what holds it once it lands:
- [[Data Source]], the four origins a record can arrive from, separated on who is answerable for the quality, whether malformed input is the default, and when the data has to be processed
- [[Storage Engine]], the layer that owns the bytes on disk and the paths that reach them, told apart from the database around it by a pluggable server on one side and an embedded library with no query language at all on the other

How it is encoded, and which way the table is flattened, in an `Encoding/` area that did not exist before this chapter:
- [[Data Serialization]], the round-trip contract and the three axes (human readability, text against binary, access pattern) that settle any format by inspection, with the six formats of the chapter's own comparison plus BSON as instances of it
- [[Row-Major and Column-Major Order]], the access-pattern axis stated in full, with the offset arithmetic, the fraction-of-columns ratio that has no row count in it, and the cache line as the reason contiguous beats strided

What shape the records are organized into, in a `Representation/` area that did not exist before this chapter:
- [[Data Model]], the three families on five checkable axes, with the document and graph models carried as subsections rather than notes
- [[Relational Model]], the relation as a set of tuples with what follows from "set", and declarativity priced as the optimizer's freedom to change steps the query never named
- [[Database Normalization]], functional dependency and the normal forms built on it, with what the decomposition buys and what the joins cost
- [[Structured Data]], the schema-or-not pair on one note, with semi-structured as the middle case and the asymmetry against the warehouse and lake split spelled out

What a database promises about a group of writes:
- [[ACID]], the four guarantees, each with its stated limit, and BASE as the counterpart acronym rather than as an absence of reasoning about correctness

When the computation runs, in a `Processing/` area that did not exist before this chapter:
- [[Batch Processing]], the bounded-input contract and everything that follows from the input being fixed before the run starts
- [[Stream Processing]], the unbounded-input contract, the stateful-computation argument with the factor-of-window arithmetic, and what that factor is paid for

How two processes that do not share memory exchange data. These three went to `Knowledge/Systems Architecture/Distribution/` rather than to Data Systems, on the same reasoning that sent three of the four requirements across in [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design|DMLS chapter 2]]: the three modes are three answers to a question any software system asks regardless of what it stores, and [[Microservices]] already owned the request-driven half in that domain, so writing them into Data Systems would have put a second copy of an existing note behind a domain boundary:
- [[Modes of Data Flow]], the three modes on five questions apiece, carrying the store mode in full and handing the other two to notes of their own
- [[Request-Driven Communication]], the four clauses of a synchronous call and the availability product that follows from the second of them
- [[Event-Driven Communication]], the same contract with three clauses changed, and the retention window as the bound on the promise that a consumer need not be running

Amended, not produced, by this chapter:
- [[Transaction]], whose single deferral to [[DDIA]] chapter 8 was split in two, because this chapter delivered the guarantees half many chapters of book time early: what rides on the boundary is now named and handed to [[ACID]], only concurrency and isolation levels are still deferred, and the `## Formal statement` stopped being a discharge and became the commit-or-abort contract with no third outcome
- [[Online Transaction Processing]], which gained the workload-to-layout association, that wanting whole records by key is what makes the layout underneath row-major rather than a separate design decision, and the availability requirement that comes of somebody waiting on the answer
- [[Online Analytical Processing]], which gained the link to the note that finally defines the columnar storage every one of its downstream choices had been leaning on
- [[Hybrid Transactional-Analytical Processing]], which gained three convergence products, one of which turned out to be a category error: Apache Iceberg is a table format with no engine and no catalog, so its atomic metadata-pointer swap is a transactional property belonging to a file layout rather than to a database
- [[Extract-Transform-Load]], which gained the full list of what a transform step actually does and the broader set of destinations a load step can write to
- [[Data Warehouse]] and [[Data Lake]], which each gained one clause reconciling structured against unstructured with the schema-timing axis they were already separated on, keeping the timing as the sharper statement and the schema-or-not as the upstream property; the lake additionally handed its format roster away to [[Data Serialization]]
- [[Data Engineering]], the note the chapter is named after, which gained the scope of the substrate the role owns, where the records come from, what holds them, and whether they move in scheduled bulk or one at a time
- [[Microservices]], which gained the pointer naming its service-to-service calls as the request-driven mode and routing to where that mode's contract and its alternative are set out
- [[Model]], [[Node]], [[Min-Max Scaling]], [[Open Data Repositories]], [[Batch Learning]] and [[Online Learning]], each of which gained the sentence separating its sense of a word from the sense this chapter brought: the fitted function against the data model, the protocol participant against the graph vertex, rescaling a column against decomposing a relation, where to obtain a dataset against where a running system's data arrives from, the training regime against the bounded job, and the update rule against computation over data in motion
- [[Feature]], which gained the static and dynamic split as vocabulary, a static feature computed by a scheduled job over data at rest and a dynamic one computed off a transport as records arrive, with the full treatment, how each kind is computed and why the two pipelines must agree on the value they produce, arriving in [[DMLS Ch07 Model Deployment and Prediction Service|DMLS chapter 7]] and living in the same note, and the store that serves both kinds still [[DMLS]] chapter 10's
- [[Poor-Quality Data]], which gained the link to the upstream account of where malformed data comes from, since the origins differ in how likely they are to arrive wrong and in who is answerable when they do

Hubs and indexes revised to match what the chapter delivered, rather than notes it produced:
- [[Data Systems]], whose scope paragraph no longer claims the domain is one chapter old from one book, and which gained three areas, `Encoding/`, `Representation/` and `Processing/`
- [[Systems Architecture]], whose note count and book count were restated and whose `Distribution/` area gained three notes

## Open questions

- The document and graph models were deliberately not given notes, and the deferral is worth stating as a deferral rather than leaving to be inferred from their absence. Both are `###` subsections inside [[Data Model]], because the reading gives them eight lines and four lines against the relational model's sixteen with exact conditions attached, and because [[DDIA]] chapter 3, "Data Models and Query Languages", is a chapter-length treatment of what this chapter gives twenty lines to. Every note in the `Representation/` block was therefore written to be amended rather than to settle anything: [[Data Model]]'s five-axis table is the frame the deeper treatment will be hung on and not the treatment, [[Relational Model]] says outright that its query-language half is a sketch, and [[Database Normalization]] stops at third normal form and Boyce-Codd with the higher forms named and not stated. What that chapter will settle is whether the document and graph models take notes of their own, which aliases move off [[Data Model]] when they do, and whether the locality argument for documents survives the case where the relationships turn out not to be rare after all.
- The format table's provenance, which is the first time this vault has had to tell two kinds of artifact apart. The raw reading notes carry a bare Wikipedia URL, the comparison of data-serialization formats, as a bullet immediately above the six-row table, and that adjacency is the strongest evidence available about where the table came from. Every row was re-derived from the format's own specification for [[Data Serialization]], RFC 8259 and ECMA-404 for JSON, the Informational RFC 4180 for CSV, the Parquet and Avro specifications, the Protocol Buffers documentation and CPython's own, and the URL was dropped rather than cited, because Wikipedia is not a primary source for a factual table. What is worth recording is the distinction that forced the work: a table read off a comparison page is a different kind of artifact from a claim read out of a chapter, since the chapter claim is the author's and can be attributed to the book, while the comparison row is an anonymous aggregation whose only honest handling is to go to what it aggregates. Nothing in [[Quality Bar]] says this, and no chapter settles it; it is a rule this vault owes itself.
- Latency and throughput are still unowned, and this chapter does not settle it. [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] left them open and said they are owed by the first chapter that states one of them precisely enough to check a claim against, and [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design]] reported that its chapter contained neither word. This one does contain `latency`, three times in the raw reading, and every use is qualitative: data from users must be processed quickly because somebody is waiting, passing data through a database is unsuitable for applications with strict latency requirements, and stream processing is often low latency because a record is handled as soon as it is generated. Three qualitative uses do not define a term, so the debt is not discharged, and this run says so explicitly rather than by silence. `throughput` does not appear at all. [[DDIA]] chapter 2 is still where both words are expected.
- Whether [[Extract-Transform-Load]] belongs in `Analytics/` now that `Processing/` exists. It is a batch pipeline and the new folder is named for exactly that, so the argument for moving it is not weak. What holds it where it is is that its subject is the copy that feeds the warehouse rather than the shape of the computation, and its `up` is [[Data Warehouse]], which is a claim about what it is part of and not about what kind of thing it is. It stays in `Analytics/` this run, on the rule that a folder move is not a side effect of a chapter that did not raise the question. The trigger is [[DDIA]] chapter 11, which is titled "Batch Processing" and will amend both the folder's contents and this note's subject at once.
- The product-roster question, answered here as a rule and applied to only one of the two notes it bears on. [[DDIA Ch01 Trade-Offs in Data Systems Architecture]] asked whether a product roster belongs inside a concept note at all, and said it would be decidable the next time a chapter arrived carrying one of its own, naming [[DDIA]] chapter 4. This chapter arrived first and carried several, so the rule was settled here: a roster belongs in the note whose subject is the category the products instantiate, as a named subsection, and it says what each product is an instance of rather than describing the product; it never supplies `## Formal statement` content, which [[Quality Bar]] already rules out, since a vendor comparison cannot be found false the way a contract can; and a roster sitting in a note about something else is duplication and moves. It was applied immediately to [[Data Lake]], whose format roster moved to [[Data Serialization]], where the category is serialization formats and each entry is an instance of the round-trip contract that note states. It was not applied to [[Cloud-Native Architecture]], whose twelve-product roster is the case that raised the question in the first place, because that is a different domain and a different book, and re-auditing it as a side effect of this run would have been a silent restructuring of a note nothing here touched. That note still owes the audit.
- `SQL` is claimed provisionally. [[Relational Model]] takes it as an alias because a reader typing it today should land somewhere that explains what the statements are addressed to, and nothing else in the vault is closer. The claim is too wide on its face: SQL is a language with a standard, a procedural extension in every implementation and semantics that depart from the relational model in places the model has no opinion about, starting with duplicate rows and nulls. [[DDIA]] chapter 3 is titled "Data Models and Query Languages", so it is the chapter that may give SQL a note of its own and take the alias back.
- `Machine Learning/Operations/` is reported on and not reopened. The standing position from [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] is that nothing moves, with two triggers, the folder passing 25 notes or [[DMLS]] chapter 10 arriving. The folder still holds four notes, this chapter added nothing to it, and neither trigger fired.
- [[Modes of Data Flow]] is the note in this run that fits its kind worst, and the strain is recorded rather than acted on, since no new `note_kind` was proposed and the evidence for one is a count rather than a single case. The note is a `concept` that behaves like an `index` for two thirds of its length and like an ordinary concept for the remaining third: two of its three members have notes of their own and are characterized in a clause each, while the third, passing data through a store, has no note and is carried inside it in full. `index` cannot express that, because its shape assumes every member is either written elsewhere or named as unwritten, and there is no provision for a member the index is itself the treatment of. Writing a fourth note for the store mode would have fixed the shape and produced a note with one paragraph in it, which is worse. The same pattern shows up weakly three more times in this run, in notes whose titles carry two subjects because neither is definable without the other: [[Row-Major and Column-Major Order]], [[Structured Data]] with unstructured data living on it, and [[ACID]] with BASE under `## VS`. None of those three strains against `concept`, since a contrast is a legitimate thing for a concept note to be about, and they are noted here only because they are the same shape seen from a different angle. What accumulates is the case that this vault has notes whose job is to hold a distinction rather than to define a thing, and if that case is ever argued, these four are the evidence.
- Three new areas landed in one domain in one chapter, `Encoding/`, `Representation/` and `Processing/`, taking [[Data Systems]] from four areas to seven, and nothing has tested whether that is the right cut. The seam most likely to be wrong is between `Encoding/` and `Representation/`, since [[Data Serialization]] and [[Data Model]] are both answers to "what shape is this data in", separated on whether the answer is about bytes or about records, and that separation is clean today only because [[Row-Major and Column-Major Order]] was placed with the bytes. It is a note about a layout, which is a byte-level fact, with `up: "[[Data Serialization]]"`, and it would read equally well as a note about how a model is realized. The check is whether the next chapter that adds to either area adds to both, and [[DDIA]] chapters 3 and 4, on data models and on storage and retrieval, are the pair that will show it.

Settled here, having been asked earlier:

- [[DDIA Ch01 Trade-Offs in Data Systems Architecture]] scheduled the [[Model]] against data model collision for [[DDIA]] chapter 3, writing that "[[Model]] is the fitted hypothesis, and [[DDIA]] chapter 3 brings data models". **Settled**, and by a different book than the one expected. [[Data Model]] exists and is titled for the qualified phrase rather than the bare word, both notes carry the separating sentence, and neither claims `model` as an alias, so a reader typing it lands by filename on the fitted function and is told in the first paragraph where the other sense lives. Chapter 3 of [[DDIA]] will now deepen a note rather than create one and adjudicate a collision.
- The same note recorded [[Node]] as a bare and heavily overloaded title, with a decision-tree node scheduled to arrive from [[HOML]] chapter 6 and one of the two expected to have to be qualified. **Partly settled**, and not in the order it was framed in. The second collision on that word landed first, from the graph data model, and it was resolved without [[Node]] giving up anything, because the new material never claims the word: [[Data Model]] carries the vertex inside its graph subsection and [[Node]] gained one sentence saying that a vertex is a stored record with no protocol role and no machine behind it. That is evidence the title can survive a collision, and it is not evidence it survives the next one, since a decision tree node is a far more commonly typed sense than a graph vertex. The [[HOML]] chapter 6 collision is still ahead.
- The same note named batch processing and stream processing as the largest deliberate deferral of its own chapter, building blocks it used without defining. **Retired, both halves**, by [[Batch Processing]] and [[Stream Processing]], which are written as one pair on one axis, whether the input ends. Of that note's list of five, database and search index remain undefined, and the cache already had its treatment inside [[Derived Data]] as the worked example of rebuildability.
- The product-roster question from the same note. **Answered as a rule, applied in part**, as the open question above sets out: the rule is settled and [[Data Lake]] was corrected under it, while [[Cloud-Native Architecture]], the note that raised it, still owes the audit.
- [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] on latency and throughput. **Not settled**, and the evidence is stated rather than left to silence: `latency` appears three times in this chapter and every use is qualitative, `throughput` does not appear at all.

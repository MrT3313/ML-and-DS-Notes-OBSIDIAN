## Thesis

Every chapter of this book so far has taken the training set as a thing that exists, and this one asks where it came from. It answers in three movements that are really three stages of one act: drawing the rows, getting labels onto them, and repairing what the draw turned out to be once somebody looks at it. Read as a list it is a survey, a folder of sampling procedures, five routes to a label, two families of remedy for a target whose classes are not present in comparable numbers, one way of not collecting data at all, and one operation with two masters. Read as an argument it is much tighter than that, because the same sentence is being made at every level, and the sentence is that every remedy in the chapter is paid for somewhere else. Nonprobability sampling buys speed and a project that starts this week, and pays in a bias that cannot be estimated from inside the sample, since the evidence about the bias is exactly the units that were never drawn and a larger sample is a more precise estimate of the wrong thing. Weak supervision buys scale, turning one expert hour into a heuristic that runs over a million rows instead of into a hundred labels, and pays in label noise that a denoising model can estimate only where the functions disagree, which is why three spellings of one heuristic are the failure it is least able to see. Active learning buys label efficiency and pays by destroying the randomness of its own labelled pool, so the set it produces is one no honest held-out estimate can be computed on and is tied to the model class that did the selecting. Resampling buys an optimizer that is no longer paid to answer with the majority and pays by making the training distribution one nobody will ever send you, which is why its single unrecoverable error is scoring the model on the data it rebalanced. Natural labels buy ground truth for nothing and pay in a delay that can only be shortened by declaring the label before the outcome is in, which is to say by lying about it in a way that the confusion matrix, computed under the same cutoff, faithfully records as a true negative. Even the remedies for the remedies charge, since Elkan's threshold result says a cost ratio in the objective and a count ratio in the data buy the same decision boundary, so choosing between the two families is choosing what to distort rather than choosing whether to. The second thing worth saying is about where the material landed, and it is the domain membership test read the other way round from [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals|chapter 3]]. That chapter was a machine learning book's chapter about the data in which almost nothing needed a fitted model in the sentence, so it produced no new note in `Knowledge/Machine Learning/` at all and amended seven there instead. This chapter is also almost entirely about the data, and nearly every sentence in it needs a fitted model to be stated: an uncertainty score is a property of a posterior, a focal weight is a function of the model's current output, a natural label is defined against a prediction that was served, and negative transfer is a claim about a target model being worse than it would otherwise have been. So the test was never about subject matter, it is about what has to appear in the sentence, and the same nominal subject falls on opposite sides of it in two consecutive chapters. What the chapter argues without ever saying it is that a training set is not found. It is manufactured, by a sequence of choices each of which buys something and charges for it, and the bill arrives in the model.

## Extracted

How a training set is drawn, in a `Sampling/` folder that widened from carving splits to sampling in general:
- [[Sampling]], the inclusion probability $\pi_i$ as the one quantity that sorts every procedure in the folder into two kinds, with the sampling frame held apart from the population, and the four distinct uses of the operation of which splitting is only the one that gets named first
- [[Nonprobability Sampling]], the four selection rules that stand in for the missing draw, with the Horvitz-Thompson cancellation as the exact statement of what an unknown or zero inclusion probability costs, and quota sampling separated from [[Stratified Sampling]] on the one point that decides everything
- [[Weighted Sampling]], the weights normalized rather than taken as probabilities, the correction $w_g \propto p_g / q_g$ worked through, and the effective sample size as the measure of what unequal weights cost
- [[Reservoir Sampling]], algorithm R with its invariant proved rather than asserted, which is what makes the stopping guarantee a guarantee, and the faster skip-based variants named for what they change and what they do not
- [[Importance Sampling]], the change-of-measure identity, the support condition stated as strict positivity because the form usually quoted is vacuous, and infinite variance as the failure that returns an ordinary looking number every time

How its rows come to carry labels, in a `Labeling/` folder that did not exist before:
- [[Hand Labeling]], the protocol whose ordering is most of its content, since a schema settled after labelling has begun is paid for in relabelling, and the replication $r$ as the knob that decides whether disagreement is visible at all
- [[Label Multiplicity]], Cohen's kappa with the base rate worked through to show why 92 percent agreement can be worth $\kappa = 0.158$, the two Feinstein and Cicchetti paradoxes that arrive exactly under a skewed target, and Krippendorff's alpha for the three situations kappa cannot be computed in
- [[Natural Labels]], the contract in three clauses with a table of what it buys and what it does not, closing on the claim that can be found false: what is never shown is never clicked, so the absent positive confounds dislike with never having been seen
- [[Feedback Loop Length]], the delay as a per-prediction duration with both endpoints pinned, a distribution rather than a number, and the cutoff choice priced at $1 - F(\tau)$ of the true positives mislabelled and invisible to the evaluation that shares the cutoff
- [[Weak Supervision]], the labelling function as the unit of expert effort, data programming's generative model over votes and its maximum likelihood fit with the true label marginalised out, and the sample complexity result read for what it actually substitutes
- [[Active Learning]], three scenarios rather than one loop, three uncertainty measures that agree in the binary case and disagree past two classes, and query-by-committee as an ensemble used backwards, keeping the disagreement and throwing the prediction away

What to do when the classes are not present in comparable numbers, the two families divided by what each is allowed to touch:
- [[Resampling]], the data-level answer, with the adding, removing and scheduling operators each stated exactly, and the one rule a wrong answer cannot be recovered from, that a model is never evaluated on resampled data
- [[Loss Reweighting]], the algorithm-level answer, three named techniques shown to be one weighted risk with three answers to what the weight is a function of, carrying Elkan's threshold formula and the exchange rate that makes the two families interconvertible

Reusing a model rather than collecting more data:
- [[Transfer Learning]], Pan and Yang's domain and task pair with the inequality that makes the term mean something, the four cases the inequality decomposes into and what each predicts about the supervision budget, and negative transfer as the named failure that no procedure checks for in advance

The same operation put to two purposes:
- [[Perturbation]], the budget $\rho$ carrying the label-preservation assumption that no formula can check, the fast gradient sign method with the linearity argument read off its own magnitudes, adversarial training as a saddle point, and the settled answer to whether the perturbation-based semi-supervised method is simply augmentation, which is that the operation is identical and the purpose is not

Amended, not produced, by this chapter:
- [[Random Sampling]] and [[Stratified Sampling]], both widened from carving a split to drawing a subset for any purpose, and both re-pointed `up` from [[Testing Set]] to [[Sampling]], which is the change that makes the folder a subject rather than a step. The first gained the miss probability for a rare category, $e^{-fNp}$, with the finding that what decides it is the expected number of rare-class members drawn and not the rarity or the fraction alone; the second gained the case where the strata are not a partition, and the two disagreeing scikit-learn behaviours on a multilabel target
- [[Class Imbalance]], the largest amendment after the folder itself, which gained the narrowed form of the complexity claim, the two families of remedy named by what each is allowed to touch, the no-evaluation-on-resampled-data rule, the reading of a severely rare class as a coverage problem rather than a weighting one, and the standing position that the imbalance should sometimes be left alone because the priors are what the model is supposed to learn
- [[Data Augmentation]], which gained the third branch of its own family, synthesis, with the 2022 claim about it dated and then narrowed against model collapse and the accumulate-rather-than-replace result, and which handed the perturbation branch to a note of its own
- [[Semi-Supervised Learning]], whose `## Formal statement` discharge was removed, because this chapter delivered in writing the objective that note had deferred: self-training with a confidence threshold and its named confirmation bias, and the consistency penalty under perturbation, with the assumption each family rests on stated separately
- [[Few-Shot Learning]] and [[Zero-Shot Learning]], which each gained the general case they had been describing without naming, now linked rather than glossed, the first additionally gaining the sentence that severe imbalance produces its setting without anyone choosing it
- [[Insufficient Training Data]], which gained the four standing answers to a shortage of labels as against a shortage of data, told apart by where the label comes from and how much ground truth each still demands, with the observation that only one of the four brings new information about the target into the system
- [[Training Set]], which gained the question of how its rows come to carry labels at all as a separate question from how they were drawn, and [[Training Instance]], which now states outright that it claims the bare word `sample` and that the statistical sense of that word belongs to [[Sampling]]
- [[Nonrepresentative Training Data]], which gained the procedures that cause it, with the division of labour stated on both sides, that note owning the mismatch and [[Nonprobability Sampling]] owning the collection habit, plus the modern form of the Literary Digest failure in a pretraining corpus
- [[Precision]] and [[Recall]], which each gained one clause naming the condition that makes them worth reporting, since neither of their terms can be inflated by the large true-negative count
- [[Data Source]], the one cross-domain amendment, in `Knowledge/Data Systems/`, which gained the per-record and per-label grain at which an origin has to be kept for the accountability column above it to pay off, and the separation of provenance from lineage in the stricter engineering sense

Three metric notes were checked and deliberately left unchanged, which is worth stating rather than leaving to be inferred from their absence. [[Accuracy]] would have received two things from this chapter, that a skewed target makes it unreadable and that the floor is the majority class frequency rather than chance, and both were already in it, derived and worked on the same MNIST target the rest of that folder uses. [[F1 Score]] and [[ROC Curve]] already linked [[Class Imbalance]] and needed nothing.

Hubs and indexes revised to match what the chapter delivered, rather than notes it produced:
- [[Machine Learning]], whose `Data/`, `Paradigms/` and `Objectives/` paragraphs were rewritten, the first because `Sampling/` stopped being the folder that carves a split and `Labeling/` did not exist before
- [[Home]], whose machine learning domain gloss was widened to cover obtaining and labelling the data, not only specifying, fitting and evaluating a model

## Open questions

- This chapter note does not carry the bare chapter title as its second alias, which is a deviation from the shape the three earlier chapter notes follow, and it is deliberate. Chapters 1 to 3 each carry `DMLS Chapter N` alongside the chapter's own title, and this chapter's title is "Training Data", which [[Training Set]] already claims as an alias. Obsidian resolves aliases case-insensitively, so claiming it here would silently capture links meant for a `_Foundations/` concept note and send them to a reading record instead. The vault currently has no duplicate alias claim anywhere, and this run does not introduce the first. What is left open is whether the convention should be written down as a rule, that a chapter note yields the bare title to any `Knowledge/` note already holding it, or whether the three earlier notes were simply lucky in their titles. Nothing in [[Quality Bar]] says either, and no chapter of either book settles it.

- [[Loss Reweighting]] carries a strain against its kind and the strain is recorded rather than acted on. It is a family note whose three members all multiply a loss term by a weight, which is what makes them one family, and two of the three set that weight from the label and are therefore fixed before the first gradient step, while focal loss sets it from the model's current output and is recomputed every step. That is a difference in what the weight is a function of, and it is the difference the note itself argues is load bearing, so the note is a family whose members do not all run at the same time. Focal loss is the member most likely to earn a note of its own, at which point the difficulty-weighting mechanism leaves with it and what remains is a genuinely homogeneous family of label-indexed weights. No new `note_kind` was proposed on the strength of this, and none should be: the quality bar's standing precedent for "not yet" is three accumulated strain records, and this is one.

- Whether [[Sampling]] should have been an `index` rather than a `concept`. It has six members in its folder, every one of them written, which is the condition `index` is earned by, and a reader arriving at it does want to be told what is under it. It was written as a `concept` on the [[Feature Scaling]] precedent, because it carries its own definition and its own mathematics, the inclusion probability and the probability against nonprobability split, rather than only listing what is beneath it, and an `index`'s required shape has no place to put those. The trigger is the same one that converted [[Feature Scaling]]'s relatives: if a later chapter adds enough members that the definition stops being the majority of the note, the definition becomes the scope statement and the mathematics moves to a note of its own.

- Whether `Data/Labeling/` survives the chapters that own its subject. Two are already scheduled and they pull in opposite directions. [[DMLS]] chapter 11, "The Human Side of Machine Learning", brings who does the labelling and under what conditions, which is material this folder's notes currently gesture at and decline, and it may want a home of its own rather than six amendments. [[DMLS]] chapter 8 brings the monitoring half of natural labels, working out how long you must wait before a drop in measured performance is real rather than an artifact of labels that have not landed, and that lands in `Operations/` rather than here, which splits one subject across two folders. The folder holds six notes and is coherent today. What would break it is either of those two chapters wanting a seventh that is not about how a row comes to carry a label.

- `pretraining` and `pretrained model` are claimed provisionally by [[Transfer Learning]], on the precedent by which [[Relational Model]] claimed `SQL` provisionally in [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals|chapter 3]]: a reader typing either word today should land somewhere that explains what the object is for, and nothing in the vault is closer. The claim is too wide on its face in one specific direction. [[Self-Supervised Learning]] already owns `pretext task` and is the usual way a pretrained model is obtained, so the object and the recipe for it are currently split across two notes with the object's name on the wrong one of them. A later chapter treating pretraining at length may take both aliases back.

- `Machine Learning/Operations/` is reported on and not reopened. The standing position from [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] is that the fold of MLOps into Machine Learning holds and nothing moves, with two triggers, the folder passing 25 notes or [[DMLS]] chapter 10 arriving. The folder was listed while writing this note and still holds four files, this chapter added nothing to it, and neither trigger fired. The reason it added nothing is worth stating rather than leaving as an absence, because this chapter came closer than the previous two did: [[Feedback Loop Length]] went to `Data/Labeling/` rather than to `Operations/`, on the ground that it is a property of the labelling arrangement rather than of a deployed model and is measurable before anything ships, even though the thing it bounds, the cadence of [[Continual Learning]], lives in that folder.

- Latency and throughput are still unowned, and this chapter does not settle it. [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] left both open, [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design]] reported that its chapter contained neither word, and [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals]] reported three qualitative uses of `latency` and no use of `throughput`. This chapter's raw reading notes were searched for both and contain neither, not once, which is the weakest possible result and is stated rather than left to silence. [[DDIA]] chapter 2 is still where both words are expected.

- Adversarial examples at depth are owed by nobody, and the honest record is that this is unowned rather than deferred. [[Perturbation]] defines the adversarial example against a budget, gives the one-step attack and the min-max defence, and states what the defence buys and does not, and it stops there deliberately. No chapter of either book on the shelf is named for the subject, so certified defences, the attack taxonomy and the transferability literature have no arrival date. Recording it as unowned is the point: a deferral names a chapter, and this cannot.

- Seven forward collisions were declined, each with the trigger that would settle it. Feature engineering goes to [[DMLS]] chapter 5, which the previous chapter already owed it. The static against dynamic feature split was sent there with it and does not arrive there: it arrived in [[DMLS Ch07 Model Deployment and Prediction Service|DMLS chapter 7]] and lives in [[Feature]], which now says how a deployed system computes each kind and why the two pipelines must agree on the value they produce, with the store that serves both still [[DMLS]] chapter 10's. Data distribution shift and monitoring go to [[DMLS]] chapter 8, which also owes [[Model Rot]] its `concept drift` and `data drift` aliases and which this chapter's natural-label material leans on without reaching for. A/B testing and testing in production go to [[DMLS]] chapter 9. The labelling and annotation tool market goes to [[DMLS]] chapter 10, which is why [[Hand Labeling]] dates every product line it names instead of arguing about them. The human side of annotation, and fairness, go to [[DMLS]] chapter 11. Hierarchy-aware evaluation was sent to [[DMLS]] chapter 6 and does not arrive there, that chapter having been read and mentioning no label hierarchy, so it is unowned by this book and writable from the survey [[Hierarchical Classification]] already cites. Convolution, and building an invariance into the architecture rather than demonstrating it one augmented copy at a time, goes to [[HOML]] chapter 14.

- One research item could not be settled from a primary source. The full text of Japkowicz and Stephen (2002) could not be obtained, so the narrowed form of the complexity claim now in [[Class Imbalance]], that harm under imbalance is a joint function of the imbalance level, the complexity of the concept and the training set size together with the learner, rests on Japkowicz's own lecture material and on a close secondary summary rather than on the paper. The four sub-claims and their conditions are stated in the note as what that evidence supports, which is weaker than citing the paper and honest about being so. What to check against a copy of the paper: whether the simple domains that were unharmed at every imbalance level are described as linearly separable in the paper's own words, whether the complexity measure $C = \log_2 L$ off a C4.5 tree is the paper's suggestion or a later gloss, and whether the per-subcluster size at which the error stayed below one percent is 50 as recorded.

Settled here, having been asked earlier:

- [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] recorded that transfer learning "is described in this chapter and never named", that both [[Zero-Shot Learning]] and [[Few-Shot Learning]] "settle their relation to it in prose" without either claiming the name, that "nothing owns the general case", and that "DMLS chapter 4, 'Training Data', is the trigger". **Settled.** [[Transfer Learning]] exists, states Pan and Yang's domain and task formalization with the inequality that is the condition for anything to be transferred at all, decomposes that inequality into the four cases and reads the supervision budget off them, and names negative transfer as what is not guaranteed. Both siblings now link it instead of describing it, and neither one's existing argument was rewritten to make room. The honest nuance is the parenting: both keep `up: "[[Machine Learning]]"` rather than being re-parented under the note that now holds their general case, because the formal condition being met is not the same as the mechanism being shared, and [[Few-Shot Learning]] has a second route into it, episodic training over disjoint class sets, that reuses nothing anyone would call a pretrained model. The containment is real and it is a containment of settings, not of methods.
- [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] left [[Continual Learning]]'s placement in `Operations/` to be tested, on whether it is a production practice or a fitting regime. **Not reopened.** [[Feedback Loop Length]] now bounds its achievable cadence from below, which is a new inbound link and no evidence either way about the placement, and [[DMLS]] chapter 9 still owns the question.
- [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] recorded that [[Model Rot]] holds `concept drift` and `data drift` in trust as candidates to become notes of their own. **Not reopened.** This chapter uses the decay without naming either kind, and [[DMLS]] chapter 8 still owns them.
- [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] asked whether `Machine Learning/Operations/` can carry this book. **Reported, not reopened.** The folder holds four notes, neither trigger fired, and the open question above records why a chapter this close to the folder's subject still added nothing to it.
- [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] on latency and throughput, asked again by [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design]] and by [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals]]. **Not settled**, and the evidence is the strongest negative yet: neither word appears anywhere in this chapter's raw reading notes.

## Thesis

Read from the front this is a survey, six things you can do to a column: fill it where it is blank, put it on a comparable range, reshape its distribution, cut it into buckets, turn its categories into numbers, multiply it by another column, and represent it when it was never a number to begin with. Read from the back it is an argument about a single failure, label or held-out information reaching the features in a shape that will not exist when the model is asked for a prediction. The chapter never draws the join itself, and the join is the whole of what makes it one chapter rather than two, because four of the six operations are themselves among the six named causes of the failure. A scaler stores a mean, an imputer stores a median, an encoder stores a vocabulary, a discretizer stores bucket edges: every one of those is a parameter fitted across rows, and fitting across rows means fitting across the split unless somebody stops it. So the claim being made is not that feature engineering matters, which the first line asserts and nothing afterwards argues, nor that leakage is a hazard, which is the flat version everyone already agrees with. It is that the learned state inside an ordinary preprocessing step is the mechanism, and that a model scored on a file whose transformers were fitted on the whole of it is being scored on information it will never hold. The closing section is the reply, and it is deliberately modest: judge a column by what its removal costs, which is a measurement, and by whether it is even present in data you have not seen, which is a ratio, and accept that the second of those is a rule of thumb rather than a result. The other thing worth recording is where the material landed, because it reads the domain membership test the opposite way from [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals|chapter 3]]. There, almost nothing needed a fitted model in the sentence and the run produced no new note under `Knowledge/Machine Learning/` at all. Here, almost every sentence needs a fitted model or the columns one is fitted on before it can be stated: an imputer's statistic is a fitted parameter, a collision rate is a property of an encoding a model reads, an importance is a difference of two risks, and leakage is defined against what a deployed model would hold at prediction time. Everything stayed in `Knowledge/Machine Learning/` and no neighbouring domain received anything, which is the same test giving the opposite answer on two chapters of one book.

## Extracted

What you do to a column, in `Data/Preprocessing/`, a folder that went from eight notes to ten:
- [[Feature Hashing]], new. The hashing trick as the answer to a level set that is unbounded rather than merely wide, with the hash space fixed before the level count is known, the collision probability derived by the birthday argument so that the load factor $K/2^{b}$ turns out to be the only thing that controls it, and Weinberger and colleagues' signed-hash construction, whose cross terms cancel in expectation and make the hash kernel unbiased.
- [[Feature Crossing]], new. The cross as a product of level sets, with the overfitting claim derived from the cardinality product rather than asserted beside it, since the rows supporting the average level fall multiplicatively with every parent added while the sample size stays where it was.
- [[Missing Value Imputation]], amended, and the second largest amendment in the run. It gained Rubin's three missingness conditions written as conditions on the missingness indicator rather than as three English sentences, the cut between column deletion and row deletion with the condition under which each is defensible, and the answer to a question the raw reading asked, that plausibility is not what makes a fill safe, since no statistic preserves the fact of having been missing and only a separate indicator column does.
- [[Feature Scaling]], amended. It gained the production claim: the stored constants are a description of the training distribution and nothing else, so a serving distribution that has moved leaves them describing a population that is no longer there, and no adjustment to the scaler repairs it, only a refit.
- [[Feature Distribution Transformation]], amended. It gained the quantization framing, that bucketizing shrinks an infinite set of possible values to a handful of numbers to estimate, the fact that the operation applies to columns that were already discrete, the three places bucket boundaries actually come from, and the caution that a statistic computed on a log-transformed column is a statistic about the logs.
- [[One-Hot Encoding]], amended. It gained hashing as the third response to high cardinality alongside target encoding and a learned embedding, and the distinction the whole encoding block rests on, between a level set that is large and one that is unbounded and still growing.

Representing something that was never a number, at the `Data/` top level:
- [[Embedding]], new, and the block of this chapter the vault owned in no form at all. The map into a space of fixed width with the inequality against the vocabulary size as the reason to bother, the four positional schemes with the raw index first because it is the one that does not work, the sinusoidal formula, and the derivation that makes fixed positional encoding a special case of a Fourier feature rather than an analogy for one, since the inner product of two encodings collapses to a sum over the offset alone.

The failure the operations cause, in `Data/Challenges/`, a folder that went from seven notes to eight:
- [[Data Leakage]], new. Label or held-out information reaching the features in a form unavailable at inference, stated formally as Kaufman, Rosset and Perlich's legitimacy condition with the no-time-machine requirement, six named causes, three detection habits, and the separation from [[Data Snooping Bias]] that the whole second half of the chapter depends on.

Judging a feature once it exists, at the `Data/` top level:
- [[Feature Importance]], new. Importance as the measured deterioration on removal, with the two procedures that "removal" names kept apart, refitting without the column against permuting it in held-out rows, because they answer different questions and a column can score high on one and low on the other. The Shapley value is stated rather than merely named, with the weight read as an average over arrival orders.
- [[Feature Generalization]], new. Coverage as a ratio with its denominator named, the standard error that makes a train-against-test gap checkable rather than eyeballed, and the reason a low-coverage column is only a rough rule of thumb against keeping it, which is that an MNAR blank is itself evidence.

Amended, not produced, by this chapter:

It yields its `feature crosses` alias to the note that now defines the operation, gains a `## VS` on learned against engineered features that declines to claim either of `feature learning` and `representation learning`, hands its feature-importance subsection to a note of its own, has the operations taxonomy widened so that every branch now names what this run produced, and loses a sentence that used a source as a bare subject.

It also dropped a three-way taxonomy that disagreed with [[Feature Engineering]]'s, and gained the two costs of a useless column that nobody owned, the memory to serve it and the time it adds to a request, neither of which shows anywhere in a validation score.
- [[Data Snooping Bias]], which yielded its `test set leakage` alias and gained the sentence separating the analyst from the transformer, so that snooping is selection and leakage needs nobody in the room.
- [[Generalization]], one clause telling itself apart from [[Feature Generalization]]: model error against column availability, one word over two subjects.
- [[Random Sampling]], [[Resampling]], [[Poor-Quality Data]] and [[Data Augmentation]], each gaining one clause on the leakage cause it already half held, a time-correlated split drawn uniformly, an oversampler manufacturing duplicates, near-duplicates left in before the split, and copies descended from one original landing on both sides of it.

- [[Zero-Shot Learning]] and [[Few-Shot Learning]], which each had an unowned use of "embedding space" turned into a link.
- [[Feature]], whose promise about the static and dynamic split was rewritten, and which gained the two new neighbours that judge a column, one asking what a fitted model loses without it and one asking whether it is there at all.

## Open questions

- Where [[Data Leakage]] lives. It went to `Data/Challenges/` on the folder rule, since it is a data pathology like its seven neighbours, with `up: "[[Feature Engineering]]"`, which is the activity that causes four of its six named causes. The cost is real and is named rather than hidden: it sits one folder away from [[Data Snooping Bias]] in `Evaluation/`, which is the note a reader most needs to meet beside it, and the pair is held together by the distinguishing sentence written on each side rather than by proximity in the tree. The trigger to revisit is a chapter that treats evaluation pathologies as a family and wants them filed together.

- Whether [[Feature Engineering]] should have become an `index`. Six notes now point `up` at it, which makes it the largest hub-like note outside the domain hubs, and a reader arriving at it does want to be told what is underneath. It stayed a `concept` on the [[Sampling]] precedent, because its `## Formal statement` carries the map $\phi$ and the composition $h \circ \phi$, which an `index`'s required shape has nowhere to put, and because there is no existing note the composition argument could move to. The trigger is that second condition rather than the first: the first note that could own the composition makes the conversion free, and until one exists the count is not the deciding fact.

- The `Data/` top level now holds seven notes, having gained three in one chapter, and nothing has tested whether that is a folder waiting to happen. What would decide it is whether the three new arrivals share a subject or merely share a level: [[Feature Importance]] and [[Feature Generalization]] are two readings taken on the same candidate column and would make a coherent pair under a name like judging a feature, while [[Embedding]] belongs with neither and sits at the top level because it is the extraction branch of the parent activity. The obvious cut, an embeddings folder, was declined this run on two notes of evidence, [[Embedding]] and [[Feature Hashing]], and because [[HOML]] chapters 13 and 16 own the architectures that produce embeddings and may want a different name for the folder they arrive into.

- `Data/Preprocessing/` now holds ten notes. Its split was investigated by [[HOML Ch02 End-to-End Machine Learning Project]] and deliberately deferred to [[HOML]] chapter 13, with the open part being which axis to cut on rather than whether to cut, and this run did not reopen it. The size is reported and the deferral stands.

- Latency and throughput, asked now for the fifth consecutive chapter and still unowned, and the record here is easy to get wrong so it is stated exactly. `latency` appears **once** in this chapter's raw reading notes, at line 125, as one of five costs of carrying too many features, in the phrase about inference latency increasing. `throughput` does not appear at all. One bare mention inside a list is not a definition, so the debt is not discharged. This is **weaker** than [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals]], which reported three qualitative uses of `latency`, and stronger only than [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design]] and [[DMLS Ch04 Training Data]], which reported neither word. [[DDIA]] chapter 2 is still where both are expected.

- `Machine Learning/Operations/` is reported on and not reopened. The standing position from [[DMLS Ch01 Overview of Machine Learning Systems]] is that the fold of MLOps into Machine Learning holds and nothing moves, with two triggers, the folder passing 25 notes or [[DMLS]] chapter 10 arriving. The folder was listed while writing this note and still holds four files, and neither trigger fired. What this chapter did send that way is a link rather than a note: [[Feature Scaling]]'s stale constants are [[Model Rot]] arriving at the preprocessing step, and [[Continual Learning]] is named as the practice that answers it.

- Terms deliberately left unclaimed, each with what would settle it. `fourier features` and random feature maps, described in [[Embedding]] to the extent the positional derivation needs and no further, owed to a kernel-methods chapter, [[HOML]] chapter 5. `Shapley value` as the game-theoretic object, stated in [[Feature Importance]] as an importance and not as a value of a cooperative game, owed to mathematics or to [[DMLS]] chapter 11. `time-based split`, `temporal split` and `split by time`, unclaimed because a split by time is not random sampling wearing a different hat and earned no note in that run, and not owed to [[DMLS]] chapter 6 after all: that chapter was read and does not treat splitting, while chapter 5's own text does say to split by time rather than randomly, so the term is unowned with its source already read rather than deferred forward. The leakage half of it is in [[Data Leakage]] as a time-correlated split drawn uniformly. `technical debt`, which is wider than the maintenance cost [[Irrelevant Features]] owns. Bare `coverage`, which has at least three rival established meanings nearby, a confidence interval's coverage probability, the share of code a test suite exercises, and the share of a catalogue a recommender reaches. Bare `leakage` and bare `generalization`, both ambiguous and belonging to neither side of their collisions.

- `feature learning` and `representation learning` cannot be claimed by this vault yet, and [[Feature Engineering]]'s `## VS` says so in writing rather than claiming quietly or staying silent. The reason is that a note defining either would have to define it against a model family the vault has no note for: there is no deep learning note anywhere under `Knowledge/`. The trigger is the first chapter that supplies one.

- "Coverage" as a term of art. Research found no peer-reviewed definition of the word in the sense this chapter uses it; the same ratio is entirely standard under other names, as the missingness rate in the statistics literature and as completeness in the data-quality literature, where Wang and Strong's dimensions are the standing reference. [[Feature Generalization]] therefore glosses the word rather than implying it is established, and says so at the front. Worth flagging for whether a later chapter uses it consistently or drops it.

- Forward collisions declined, each with its trigger. Feature stores go to [[DMLS]] chapter 10. Data distribution shift goes to [[DMLS]] chapter 8, and the staleness of a scaler's stored statistics goes with it, as does [[Model Rot]]'s outstanding debt of its `concept drift` and `data drift` aliases. Model interpretability at depth goes to [[DMLS]] chapter 11, of which SHAP and InterpretML are instruments rather than the subject, which is why [[Feature Importance]] names them with dates and declines to argue about them. Embeddings as a network learns them, and the architectures that produce them, go to [[HOML]] chapters 13 and 16. A/B testing and test in production go to [[DMLS]] chapter 9. Hierarchy-aware evaluation does not arrive in [[DMLS]] chapter 6, which was read and mentions no label hierarchy, so it is unowned by this book rather than deferred within it.

- Research items no subagent could settle from a primary source, reported plainly with what to check. Shapley's 1953 paper itself, blocked, with the formula confirmed only through Lundberg and Lee and through Aumann's survey. Breiman (2001) as published, blocked, verified instead against the January 2001 Berkeley technical report of the same manuscript. Rubin (1976) in full, paywalled, with the ignorability condition resting on the abstract. The exact regularity conditions on the MAR-against-MNAR untestability result of Molenberghs, Beunckens, Sotto and Kenward (2008). The author order of Weinberger and colleagues (ICML 2009), which differs between the arXiv listing and some proceedings listings. Bland and Altman (1996), blocked, with the substance derivable and the citation carried for provenance only. Whether binning genuinely helps more when training data is scarce, for which no measured result varying sample size was found, only a hedged conjecture in Dougherty, Kohavi and Sahami (1995). And the section-level structure of [[DMLS]] chapter 7, verified from a detailed public summary and the publisher's table of contents rather than from the book.

- One correction owed inside a note this run amended. [[Missing Value Imputation]] now attributes the three-way classification to Rubin (*Biometrika* 63(3), 1976). Rubin 1976 introduced *missing at random* and *observed at random*; the labels MCAR and MNAR and the three-way triad are Little and Rubin's. Record this as a correction still to be made, naming Seaman, Galati, Jackson and Carlin (*Statistical Science* 28(2), 2013) as the source that states the distinction explicitly.

Settled here, having been asked earlier:

- [[DMLS Ch04 Training Data]] asked whether a chapter note yielding the bare title to a `Knowledge/` note should be written down as a rule, or whether the three chapter notes before it were simply lucky in their titles, and recorded that nothing in [[Quality Bar]] said either way. **Settled.** This chapter's title is "Feature Engineering", which is the exact filename of [[Feature Engineering]], so the same yield was required a second time in two consecutive chapters and this note takes `DMLS Feature Engineering` rather than the bare title. Two instances is a convention rather than luck, and the rule is now written into `__Meta/Quality-Bar.md`, in section 2 under the `source` kind, immediately after the chapter-note shape and before the container-note shape, with both instances cited.
- [[DMLS Ch04 Training Data]] declined feature engineering and the static against dynamic feature split forward to this chapter together. **Half arrived, half corrected.** Feature engineering is this chapter. The static and dynamic split is not in it at all, so the promise, which three notes carried, was rewritten to name [[DMLS]] chapter 7 for the treatment of how a running system computes each kind and chapter 10 for the store that serves both. That treatment has since arrived: [[DMLS Ch07 Model Deployment and Prediction Service|DMLS chapter 7]] supplied it and it lives in [[Feature]], how each kind is computed and why the two pipelines must agree on the value they produce, with the store that serves both still chapter 10's.
- [[HOML Ch02 End-to-End Machine Learning Project]] asked which chapter would bring feature extraction, the one branch of [[Feature Engineering]]'s taxonomy with no note behind it, and expected [[HOML]] chapters 13 and 16. **Partly settled**, and from the other book. [[Embedding]] names the object, the space and the discrete and continuous positional cases, so the branch is no longer empty, while the architectures that learn the map remain where they were.
- [[Irrelevant Features]] said in writing that a later chapter would add the selection criteria operationalizing its conditional-independence statement. **Settled.** The discharge is gone and the criteria are in: the finite-sample importance difference as the criterion that reads the fit, empirical mutual information as the cheap model-free one that answers a weaker question, and an $\ell_1$ penalty as the route that makes selection part of the fit.
- Latency and throughput, asked by chapters 1, 2, 3 and 4. **Not settled**, and weaker than chapter 3's result rather than stronger: one bare mention of `latency` inside a list of costs, and no `throughput` at all.

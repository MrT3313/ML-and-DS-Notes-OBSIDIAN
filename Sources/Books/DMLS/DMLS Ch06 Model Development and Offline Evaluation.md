## Thesis

This is two halves the chapter never joins, and the join is the argument worth recording. The first half is how a model gets built: selecting among candidates, combining several of them into one predictor, recording what each run did, finding out why a fit is wrong, spreading the fit across machines, and handing the search itself to a search algorithm. The second half is how a model gets judged: against a floor, against constructed inputs, against the frequencies its probabilities claim, one prediction at a time, and cut into subgroups. What runs through both, and what neither half says out loud, is that a single held-out number is not enough for any decision anybody actually makes with it. It cannot say whether the candidate set was the right set, whether the predicted probability means what it says, whether the score survives being cut into subgroups, or whether the model is insensitive to the things it ought to be insensitive to. Each half answers a different one of those questions, and the chapter never states that this is what it is doing, so the halves read as a survey of practices rather than as four complements to one inadequate number. Two facts about this run rather than about the chapter belong here as well, because both are firsts. This is the first DMLS chapter whose material spread rather than concentrated: four folders received new notes, `Evaluation/`, `Models/Ensembles/`, `Optimization/` and `_Foundations/`, with single-clause visits to a dozen more, where [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals|chapter 3]] produced nothing in this domain at all and [[DMLS Ch05 Feature Engineering|chapter 5]] put everything under `Data/`. And it is the first chapter of this book where CREATE was the default action rather than AMEND, which is a fact about what the vault already held: baselines, model selection, hyperparameter search, learning curves, the ensemble idea and error analysis were all on disk and needed widening, while calibration, slice-based evaluation, behavioural testing, experiment tracking, distributed training and AutoML were owned by nothing whatever, so the chapter met a vocabulary that was strong on fitting and estimating and had almost nothing on interrogating a fitted model.

## Extracted

Ensembles, in `Models/Ensembles/`, a folder that went from one note to four:
- [[Bagging]], new, a `method`. It carries the bootstrap resample with the distinct-row fraction derived rather than quoted, $1-(1-1/n)^n \to 1-e^{-1} \approx 0.632$, and the out-of-bag estimate that falls out of its complement: the remaining $0.368n$ rows are provably unseen by a given member, so an honest held-out score is already paid for by the resampling, at no extra fits.
- [[Boosting]], new, a `method`. It carries the reweighting loop written out in seven steps, AdaBoost's exact weight update with the derivation showing that $\alpha_t$ is the value making the member's error one half under the next distribution, and the $\prod_t 2\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1-\epsilon_t)}$ training-error bound. That bound is what makes "convert weak learners to strong ones" a theorem rather than a slogan, since every factor is strictly below one whenever a member beats a coin.
- [[Stacking]], new, a `method`. Its content is a condition rather than a formula: the metalearner is fitted on out-of-fold predictions, because fitting it in-fold hands it a column that already knows the answer.

Offline evaluation, in `Evaluation/`, which went from twelve notes at its top level to seventeen:
- [[Model Calibration]], new, a `concept`. It carries the condition $P(Y=1 \mid \hat p = p) = p$ that the raw reading's counting procedure estimates without ever writing down, the reliability diagram as that conditional estimated by binning, expected calibration error, the Brier score with Murphy's decomposition, and the separation between calibration and discrimination, which is the reason an accurate model can be badly calibrated and a useless one perfectly calibrated.
- [[Selective Prediction]], new, a `concept`. Coverage against selective risk, with the selection function and the confidence function kept apart, and it owns abstention, which is the one thing neither the threshold note nor the calibration note owned: a decision threshold picks which class and a certainty threshold picks whether to answer at all.
- [[Slice-Based Evaluation]], new, a `method`. It owns the claim that an aggregate score hides a subgroup score, stated as the pooling identity $\hat P = \sum_g (n_g/n)\hat P_g$ so that the hiding is arithmetic rather than a caution, with Simpson's paradox written as the inequality it is and the worst-group minimum as the quantity that does not hide anything.
- [[Behavioral Testing]], new, a `method`. It holds the test-type family together rather than as three unrelated bullets: minimum functionality, invariance, directional expectation and the perturbation test in one table, each row fixing what is constructed and what is asserted, with the fourth named as the one that returns a number rather than a verdict.
- [[AutoML]], new, a `concept`. The soft and hard split, and the four things that have to be fixed before the problem is well posed, of which the performance estimation strategy is the one that makes the argmin an argmin over a surrogate rather than over the score.

The practice of developing a model, in `_Foundations/`, which went from nine notes to twelve, three arriving in this run across this group and the next:
- [[Experiment Tracking]], new, a `concept`.
- [[Model Debugging]], new, a `method`, and the folder's first.

Both point `up` at [[Machine Learning System]], and what they depend on is that note's claim that deployed behaviour is a joint function of code, data and the fitted artifact. Experiment tracking is that claim operationalized: it names the arguments a fit actually reads, code version, data version, configuration, seeds and environment, and states repeatability as a necessity over all five. Model debugging decomposes the same claim from the maintenance side, since its five causes of failure are five places in that joint function where the answer can go wrong without anything raising.

Model assumptions, in `_Foundations/`:
- [[Inductive Bias]], new, a `concept`, which [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design|chapter 2]] predicted by name and which carries in writing the qualification that chapter required: it is not the `bias` of [[Bias-Variance Tradeoff]], and it says so in its first sentence before anything else is claimed.

Distributed training, in `Optimization/`, which went from eleven notes to fourteen:
- [[Distributed Training]], new, a `concept`, with [[Data Parallelism]] and [[Model Parallelism]], both `method`, under it.

The cross-domain boundary is drawn rather than assumed. The architecture domain owns the machines, the interconnect and the failure model, so [[Node]], [[Distributed System]], [[High-Performance Computing]] and [[Scalability]] are linked and not duplicated, and this domain owns the gradient, the parameter copy, the batch and the update: which parameters live where, what is reduced and when, and what the effective batch size does to the learning rate.

## Open questions

- The kind strain on [[Baseline Model]]. It is a `method`, and two of the five baselines it now owns are not fitted: a human baseline measures people and an existing solution is a system you replace. So `## Hyperparameters` and `## Implementation` are actively wrong for them, and both had to be discharged in prose inside a note whose same sections are fully populated for its other material, which is the awkward part, since the discharge sits beside a table and a code block rather than in place of them. No new kind was reached for. This is the second strain record, after [[Loss Reweighting]]'s from [[DMLS Ch04 Training Data|chapter 4]], and the standing precedent in [[Quality Bar]] for "not yet" is three.

- A second, milder strain worth recording separately, because it is a conflict between two rules rather than between a note and a kind. The Quality Bar asks `## Definition` for one or two sentences, while a DIFFERENT THINGS collision verdict requires the distinguishing sentence to live in `## Definition`, where a reader arriving by the wrong link will actually meet it. [[Inductive Bias]] and [[Selective Prediction]] each needed both and ran longer than two sentences as a result, the first separating itself from [[Bias-Variance Tradeoff]] and the second from [[Association Rule Learning]]'s confidence and from the frontmatter key. The two rules pull against each other and nothing says which wins.

- Whether `_Foundations/` should have become two folders. It now holds ten vocabulary concepts plus two practice notes, and it gained its first `method`, which is the change that raises the question: everything else in there is a word the rest of the domain assumes, and a practice you carry out is a different kind of thing. A `Development/` folder was considered and declined on two notes of evidence, against `Labeling/`'s precedent of six. The trigger is a third development-practice note.

- Whether [[Ensemble Learning]] should convert to an `index`. It now has three members in its folder, which is the condition an `index` is earned by, but it carries its own variance decomposition with the correlation floor $\rho\sigma^2$, and an `index`'s required shape, a scope statement and a list, has nowhere to put mathematics. This is the [[Sampling]] and [[Feature Engineering]] situation exactly, and the trigger is the same one: the first note the decomposition could move to, not the member count.

- Folder sizes, reported and not acted on. `Evaluation/` now holds seventeen notes at its top level plus two in `Hyperparameter Search/`, which makes it the largest flat level in the domain, and `Optimization/` holds fourteen. Neither was split and nothing has tested whether either should be.

- Latency and throughput, the sixth consecutive record, stated exactly because it is easy to get wrong. `latency` appears twice in this chapter's raw reading, at lines 5 and 24, both times as `inference latency` and both times as a property a model is selected on rather than a quantity being defined. `throughput` does not appear at all. Two qualitative mentions is stronger than [[DMLS Ch05 Feature Engineering|chapter 5]]'s single one and weaker than [[DMLS Ch03 Data Engineering Fundamentals|chapter 3]]'s three, so the debt is not discharged and [[DDIA]] chapter 2 is still where both are expected.

- `Machine Learning/Operations/` reported and not reopened, for the sixth time. It holds four notes, [[MLOps]] as its index plus [[Production Machine Learning]], [[Model Rot]] and [[Continual Learning]], and neither trigger fired: not 25 notes, and not [[DMLS]] chapter 10. What is worth recording is that this was the first chapter with a real chance of moving that folder's boundary and did not. Experiment tracking was the candidate, and [[MLOps]]'s scope paragraph decides it against the folder, since a note earns a place there when it is about a model already in production rather than a model being built, and a training run is the second. The note went to `_Foundations/` instead, so that boundary and the hub sentence restating it both stand unedited.

- Hierarchy-aware evaluation, owed here by name in three notes, could not be kept. The raw reading contains nothing on label hierarchies, and the book's own chapter 6 summary and resource list mention none, so it is unowned by this book rather than deferred inside it. All three lines were rewritten, in [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design]], [[DMLS Ch04 Training Data]] and [[DMLS Ch05 Feature Engineering]]. It is not waiting on a chapter at all: Silla and Freitas's survey, already cited in [[Hierarchical Classification]] for its vocabulary, defines hierarchical precision, recall and F-measure, though it adopts them from Kiritchenko and colleagues rather than originating them, so what is owed is the writing and not the reading.

- The time-based split family could not be kept either, and for a sharper reason. Chapter 6 does not treat splitting at all, while chapter 5's own text says to split by time rather than randomly, so a promise deferred material forward that was sitting in its own chapter. The term is therefore unowned with its source already read, rather than deferred, and the leakage half of it is in [[Data Leakage]] as a time-correlated split drawn uniformly. It was not written this run, because that is chapter 5's material and not this one's.

- Terms deliberately left unclaimed, each with what would settle it. Bare `confidence`, claimed twice over, by [[Association Rule Learning]]'s $P(B \mid A)$ and by the frontmatter key on every note, which is why [[Selective Prediction]] takes the qualified names instead. Bare `slice`, since seventeen notes already use it in the gradient-batch sense, a collision in search rather than in meaning. Bare `invariance`, with four senses live across [[Data Augmentation]], [[Resampling]], [[Feature Scaling]] and [[Moment]]. Bare `artifact`, which a dozen notes use for the fitted model object and not for a file a run emits, which is why [[Experiment Tracking]] says which sense it means in its first paragraph. Bare `versioning`, whose registry half is chapter 10's. Bare `bootstrap`, a general statistical technique of which bagging is one use. Bare `bias`, with five senses already live, [[Inductive Bias]] being the sixth and claiming none of them. And `Bayesian optimization`, named once in the reading and already glossed in [[Randomized Search]], where claiming it would be false.

- Conformal prediction is raised by the book's own chapter 6 resource list and appears nowhere in the raw reading. It is named in [[Selective Prediction]] as the alternative route to a guarantee, one that attaches a set with a coverage level rather than a threshold on a score, and it is otherwise unowned, with no arrival date.

- Forward collisions declined, each with its trigger. A/B testing and test in production to [[DMLS]] chapter 9. Model registries and the tracking-tool market to [[DMLS]] chapter 10. Data distribution shift to [[DMLS]] chapter 8. Interpretability at depth to [[DMLS]] chapter 11. - Research that could not be settled, stated plainly with what to check. The published Springer versions of Breiman's bagging (1996) and random forests (2001) were blocked and were verified against his Berkeley technical reports instead. Wolpert (1992) is paywalled and was verified against the Los Alamos preprint. Freund and Schapire (1997) returned 403 and was verified from the authors' own PDF. Wolpert's 1996 no-free-lunch theorem in its general form over priors rests on secondary sources, so [[Inductive Bias]] states the uniform-average special case it derives itself. Chow's (1970) exact cost expression comes from a secondary source. Platt is cited as 1999 here and as 2000 by Lin, Lin and Weng. Holte (1993) is paginated 63-91 in the author's own copy and 63-90 in some secondary records. Whether `zero rule` predates Weka could not be established, only that the word does not occur in Holte's paper. And the four development phases were settled from two third-party section-level summaries and from Huyen's earlier public booklet rather than from chapter 6 itself, since the book's own summary does not mention them.

- A limitation of this run rather than of the chapter: no code in any note produced here was executed, because scikit-learn is not installed in the environment the notes were written in. Every signature and default was transcribed from the 1.6 documentation, and in two places where that documentation contradicts itself on a rendered default, from the 1.6 source.

Settled here, having been asked earlier:

- [[DMLS Ch02 Introduction to Machine Learning Systems Design]] recorded that "DMLS chapter 6 owes the experiment half and chapter 10 owes the registry half" of versioning and reproducibility. **Settled**, by [[Experiment Tracking]]. What it delivers is the conditions under which a run is repeatable, stated as a necessity over the five arguments a fit actually reads rather than as a list of good habits, with the honest rider attached: a complete record is necessary and not sufficient, because reductions whose order is decided by whichever worker finishes first are outside a seed's reach.
- The same note's inductive bias question named this chapter by title as the likeliest trigger and stated in advance the qualification any such note would have to carry. **Settled**, by [[Inductive Bias]], whose first sentence carries it.
- [[HOML Ch04 Training Models]] recorded that probability calibration still had no note and that nothing in the vault said how to check whether a predicted probability means what it says. **Settled**, by [[Model Calibration]], which carries both the check, the reliability diagram with expected calibration error and the Brier score, and the fix, the maps fitted on held-out scores.
- [[HOML Ch04 Training Models]] and [[HOML Ch01 The Machine Learning Landscape]] recorded the no free lunch theorem as the only chapter 1 topic with no note anywhere in the vault. **Settled**, inside [[Inductive Bias]], which states it as the reason a bias is necessary at all rather than as a curiosity, and claims `no free lunch theorem` as an alias.
- Hierarchy-aware evaluation and the time-based split family were both asked of this chapter and **answered in the negative**, with the three lines and the one line rewritten respectively. The reasons differ and are in the open questions above, at the eighth and ninth entries.
